Key Terms

Perkins V vocabulary, plainly explained

CTE Participant

Any 9th–12th grade student who successfully completes one or more state-approved CTE course(s).

CTE Concentrator

A participant who progresses further into a Program of Study — typically by completing multiple sequenced courses within a pathway.

Program of Study (POS)

A coordinated, non-duplicative sequence of academic and technical content, spanning secondary and postsecondary education, aligned to employer-validated standards.

CLNA

Comprehensive Local Needs Assessment — the data-driven process every consortium repeats to identify and prioritize needs before writing its annual application.

S–R vs. Funding POS

"State-Recognized" (S–R) programs meet Minnesota's full quality bar; "Funding" programs are Perkins-funded while working toward full state recognition.

Basic vs. Reserve funds

Basic funds cover core Perkins-eligible activities across all CLNA elements. Reserve funds are a smaller, restricted pool for performance gaps or POS/CTE program development.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is Perkins V?

Perkins V is the common name for the Strengthening Career and Technical Education for the 21st Century Act, the federal law that funds Career and Technical Education nationwide. It requires local consortia to complete a needs assessment, identify Programs of Study, and report annually on performance.

Who administers Perkins funding in Minnesota?

Minnesota State is the sole state agency authorized to receive and disburse federal Perkins funds (Minn. Stat. 136F.79). An agreement with the Minnesota Department of Education governs the split between secondary and postsecondary CTE programs (Minn. Rule 3505.1700). Minnesota organizes the work through 23 regional consortia, each pairing school districts with a Minnesota State college.

When does the Perkins award year run?

The award period runs July 1 through June 30. The Annual Performance Report (APR) covering the prior year is due October 15, and a new Local Application is submitted each cycle.

How many Programs of Study must a consortium offer?

Minnesota requires each consortium to identify at least six state-recognized Programs of Study spanning at least four different career fields, with no more than one program brokered from an outside consortium. Dakota County currently offers 26 Programs of Study across seven career fields.

Why are the Dakota County and South Metro consortia merging?

Dakota County Technical College (Dakota County's postsecondary partner) and Inver Hills Community College (South Metro's postsecondary partner) are merging into a single institution. Because a Perkins consortium can't share a postsecondary partner with another consortium, the two consortia must also combine. See the full merger explainer.

Who is the CTE Connections Liaison?

A shared consortium position that bridges CTE classrooms with outside industry — coordinating guest speakers, tours, work-based learning placements, and equipment alignment, and participating in every CTE advisory board across the consortium. Noelle Meyer currently holds this role.

How does a district request Perkins funding for equipment or activities?

CTE teachers submit requests through a shared Google Form describing the item, cost, and how it aligns with the current Local Application. The Secondary Perkins Consortium Coordinator checks alignment to the application and CLNA, the full consortium reviews the request at a monthly meeting, and approved requests requiring state sign-off are submitted to MDE.

What documents make up a complete Local Application?

Statements of Assurance, the CLNA Results & Priorities document, the S–R POS – Funding POS spreadsheet, a combined secondary/postsecondary budget, a consolidated equipment inventory, a Perkins-Funded Positions spreadsheet with position descriptions for every funded role, and (when applicable) an improvement plan.
