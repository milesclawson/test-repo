Comprehensive Local Needs Assessment

Perkins V requires every consortium to identify its highest-priority needs, back them with data, and commit to measurable outcomes. Here's what that looks like for Dakota County.

How the CLNA was built

The consortium used a multi-pronged approach to gather and analyze data from everyone with a stake in Career and Technical Education across Dakota County. Because this was also a secondary program-approval year, the CLNA work aligned naturally with long-term planning at each district — several of which had seen administrative turnover since CTE programs were last approved five years earlier.

Inputs included Real-Time Talent labor market data, data from local governments and Chambers of Commerce, secondary secure reports, structured conversations with the Dakota-Scott County Youth Workforce Committee, interviews with superintendents and principals, and surveys of district and school leaders, CTE and non-CTE teachers, CTE and non-CTE students, families, local workforce representatives, industry advisory board members, and postsecondary faculty and students.

📋

Required consultation, per Perkins V Section 134

Federal law requires the CLNA process to include CTE teachers, counselors, and administrators; postsecondary CTE faculty; workforce development board and industry representatives; parents and students; representatives of special populations; agencies serving out-of-school, homeless, and at-risk youth; and Tribal representatives where applicable.

Baseline Data

Where the data pointed

The consortium's January 2024 Performance Report showed actual performance above Minnesota's State Determined Performance Levels in every category for three straight years — but with several concerning downward trends.

CTE Participants

4,730 in 2021 → 4,698 in 2024. Only 53% went on to become concentrators.

Math Proficiency

Secondary CTE students: 33.19% (2021) → 26.35% (2024).

Reading Proficiency

Secondary CTE students: 54.05% (2021) → 42.71% (2024).

Concentrator Gender Gap

47% of participants are female, but only 39% of concentrators are.

Programs of Study

Narrative 2 — Size, entry/exit points, and equipment

Size

Grow the number of secondary & postsecondary CTE participants and concentrators

Secondary participants declined from 4,730 (2021) to 4,698 (2024); just 53% became concentrators.
Strategies
  • Expand career exploration for middle schoolers and 9th/10th graders
  • Redesign high school career & college fairs around pathways and employability skills
  • Ensure access to industry-standard equipment and experiences
  • Support master scheduling that widens access to multiple CTE pathway courses
  • Target postsecondary outreach to low-concentrator pathways (e.g. administrative support, web & communication design)
Measurable outcomes by 6/30/26
  • Secondary participants to 4,933 (+5% from 4,698)
  • Secondary concentrator rate to 58% (+5 points from 53%)
  • +5% concentrators in targeted Programs of Study
Entry & Exit Points

Give students more ways into and out of Programs of Study

Newly approved secondary programs mean more diverse postsecondary and career pathway options — if students discover them early enough.
Strategies
  • Communicate more intentionally with counselors about program access
  • Partner with local industry to clarify and promote pathways
  • Strengthen outreach to middle-school families and administrators
Measurable outcomes
  • Redesigned high school career & college fairs by 6/30/26
  • CTE outreach to a middle school in every member district by 6/30/26
  • Clarified entry/exit points in course registration guides by 1/31/26
Equipment

Modernize industry-standard equipment, especially in Health Sciences

Updated program approvals and the DCTC/Inver Hills merger both call for secondary and postsecondary equipment to align more closely with industry standards.
Strategies
  • The CTE Connections Liaison bridges programs with outside industry to understand equipment needs
  • Every member reviews its CTE inventory and purchases to match industry needs
  • DCTC hosts consortium CTE staff for cross-district planning
Measurable outcomes by 6/30/26
  • One industry field trip & one guest speaker per CTE program, per high school
  • Larger, more diverse CTE Advisory Committees
  • Perkins funds directed to equipment aligned with approved Programs of Study

Workforce Collaboration

Narrative 3 — Local workforce boards & agencies

Industry Alignment

Keep pace with Dakota County's fast-changing industry landscape

Housing growth and major new employers, including a Meta (Facebook) data center development, are reshaping the local labor market.
Strategies
  • Fund a dedicated staff member (CTE Connections Liaison) to build relationships with businesses, Chambers of Commerce, and government partners
  • Guarantee each approved program at least one field trip, speaker, or work experience per year
Measurable outcomes
  • Every Program of Study reaches at least 3 points of contact with local industry
  • Every monthly consortium meeting includes speaker/field-trip/opportunity updates

The consortium builds on the 2024–25 Reality Store and Reverse Job Fair (with Dakota County CareerForce and DEED Vocational Rehabilitation Services) and the ISD 917 DCALS Pathways Fair, both run in partnership with the Dakota-Scott County Youth Workforce Committee.

Integrated Academic & Technical Skills

Narrative 4 — Math, reading, and credentials

Math

Reverse the decline in secondary math proficiency

33.19% (2021) → 26.35% (2024)
Key strategies
  • Personal finance & financial literacy integration in CTE Business courses
  • Cross-department PD on grade-level and industry-specific math standards
  • DCTC math tutors, transitioning from Perkins to general funds over three years

Goal: 27.67% proficiency by 6/30/26.

Reading

Reverse the decline in secondary reading proficiency

54.05% (2021) → 42.71% (2024)
Key strategies
  • PD on grade-level and industry-specific reading standards
  • Real-world writing & oral communication built into CTE curriculum

Goal: 44.85% proficiency by 6/30/26.

Quality

Increase postsecondary credentials earned by secondary & postsecondary students

Strategies
  • CTE Connections Liaison surveys local industry on credentials needed for origin, gateway, and target occupations
  • Build a scope-and-sequence of certifications from beginner to advanced, secondary through postsecondary
Measurable outcome
  • Establish a data tracking system for postsecondary credentials earned by secondary students by 12/31/26

Special Populations

Narrative 5 — Closing access & performance gaps

Equity

Address participation and performance gaps for special populations

Male CTE concentrators (38.9%) outpace female concentrators (29.4%); postsecondary students with disabilities, single parents, and out-of-workforce individuals trail the 67.04% college concentrator average by 10–21+ points.
Strategies
  • CTE Connections Liaison connects with businesses and organizations led by or advocating for underrepresented groups
  • PD on demographic analysis, bias, and cultural responsiveness for CTE staff
  • Increased busing funds to remove transportation barriers to field trips
  • DCTC tutors and an Office of Accessibility Resources liaison for special-population students
Measurable outcomes by 6/30/26
  • District CTE demographics move closer to overall district demographics
  • 2% improvement in performance gaps for students with disabilities, single parents, and out-of-workforce individuals

WBL

Diversify and expand work-based learning opportunities

Approved Work-Based Learning programs already run in 7 of 9 member districts; 5 include ACTE-Special Education programming.
Strategies

Measurable outcomes by 6/30/26

Early Postsecondary Credit

Narrative 7 — Concurrent enrollment & contract courses

Concurrent Enrollment

Grow postsecondary credits earned during high school

DCTC already delivers Welding, Math for Trades, Anatomy & Physiology, Medical Terminology, and Nursing Assistant courses at member high schools, with room to expand.
Strategies
  • Clarify concurrent-enrollment teacher licensure requirements with district HR
  • Improve family and counselor communication about concurrent-enrollment benefits
  • DCTC-funded outreach, mentoring, and required annual concurrent-enrollment PD
Measurable outcome
  • +5% secondary participation in early college programs by June 2026

Current examples: SSD 6 (Business with Inver Hills Community College), ISD 197 (College Success Strategies via IHCC; Job Search Skills via DCTC), ISD 192 (Intro to Computer Systems via University of Minnesota), ISD 200 (Business, FCS & Metals via Chippewa Valley Technical College).

Support to Professionals

Narrative 8 — Recruiting & training CTE staff

Credentialing

Grow the number of CTE staff with concurrent-enrollment credentials

Strategies
  • Work with postsecondary partners and district HR to map credentialing pathways

Goal: Every CTE teacher understands concurrent-enrollment requirements by 6/30/26.

Training

Deepen training as new five-year program approvals take effect

Strategies
  • Perkins 101 training and mentorship for new and existing leadership, starting fall 2026
  • Postsecondary staff attend NACEP and related conferences

Goal: 80% of secondary CTE staff in Perkins PD.

Performance Gaps

Narrative 9 — Gender, race/ethnicity & socioeconomic diversity

Gender

Address gender imbalances

Female students are overrepresented in Human Services (75%) and Health Sciences (86%), underrepresented in Engineering, Manufacturing & Technology (7%) and Business, Management & Administration (36%).

Race & Ethnicity

Increase racial/ethnic diversity

In the five largest programs, White students make up 63–85% of participants even where four member districts are majority students-of-color.

Socioeconomic

Increase socioeconomic diversity

District free/reduced lunch rates range 20–57%, but the five largest CTE programs range only 20–35%.

Shared strategy across all three: CTE Connections Liaison outreach to underrepresented-led businesses and organizations, plus staff PD on bias and cultural responsiveness.

Merger

Plan a joint governance structure with the South Metro Perkins Consortium

Strategies

Reserve Funds

Narrative 11 — Targeted investment beyond the base allocation

Recruitment

Recruit more CTE professionals

Many teachers hold Out-of-Field Placement licenses requiring extra graduate coursework. Strategy: outreach to appropriately-licensed candidates and PELSB/MN State partnership.

Training

Train CTE professionals on new standards

As new five-year program approvals take hold, cross-district PD supports implementation of MDE CTE frameworks.

Equipment

Health Sciences & AFNR equipment

Reserve funds target expansion of the Health Sciences program at SSD 6 and the Natural Resources, Energy & Environmental Service Systems pathway at ISD 192, ISD 659, and ISD 195, purchased by January 2026.
