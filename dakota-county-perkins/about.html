Governance & representation

How decisions get made, who represents each district, and how the consortium's leadership structure works.

How the consortium is governed

The Dakota County Perkins Consortium has established a governance model that engages a representative from each secondary district in continuous learning, collaborative decision-making, and a shared understanding of the strengths and needs of every member community.

Monthly meetings are planned and led by the Secondary Perkins Consortium Coordinator, and the consortium rotates its in-person meetings among member districts so representatives can see Perkins-funded equipment, programs, and students in action, and exchange ideas about space, instructional materials, and scheduling.

Each district determines its own process for choosing who represents it on the consortium team. Every funding request is reviewed together at a monthly meeting, checked against the current application, the CLNA, and prior-year decisions.

Minnesota's governance rules

  • Every consortium includes at least one eligible school district and one eligible college.
  • No school district or college may belong to more than one consortium.
  • Superintendents and the college president identify their Perkins coordinators.
  • The consortium identifies secondary and postsecondary fiscal hosts.
  • Each consortium submits a single annual application for funding.
  • Spending authority sits with the defined consortium leadership structure and may not be delegated.

Perkins V Basics

What Perkins V is

The law

Perkins V — formally the Strengthening Career and Technical Education for the 21st Century Act — is the federal law that funds Career and Technical Education. It requires eligible recipients to complete a Comprehensive Local Needs Assessment, identify state-recognized Programs of Study, and report annually on secondary and postsecondary performance indicators.

Who administers it in Minnesota

Minnesota State is the sole state agency authorized to receive and disburse federal Perkins funds (Minn. Stat. 136F.79). An agreement with the Minnesota Department of Education governs how funds are split between secondary and postsecondary CTE programs (Minn. Rule 3505.1700). Minnesota organizes this work through 23 regional Perkins consortia statewide.

Dakota County Perkins Consortium

A Minnesota Perkins V local consortium linking nine Dakota County school districts, BlueSky Online, and Dakota County Technical College to strengthen Career and Technical Education for every student.
