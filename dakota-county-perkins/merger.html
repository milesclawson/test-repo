Why two Perkins consortia are becoming one

A college merger is driving a consortium merger. Here's the full picture: what's changing, why, and how Dakota County and South Metro are planning the transition together.

The trigger: DCTC + Inver Hills are merging

Minnesota State announced during Winter/Spring 2025 that Dakota County Technical College and Inver Hills Community College will merge into a single institution. Minnesota's Perkins consortium definition does not allow two consortia to share one postsecondary institution — so a college merger forces a consortium merger.

What that means here

DCTC is Dakota County's postsecondary partner. Inver Hills Community College is South Metro's. Once the colleges combine, Dakota County and South Metro can no longer operate as separate Perkins consortia — they have to become one.

Before the Merger

Two consortia, two colleges, two structures

The two consortia currently run independently — separate CLNAs despite serving overlapping geography, different informal protocols, and different organizational structures.

The Plan

Proposed merger structure

Presented jointly by Minnesota State Career and Technical Education and the Minnesota Department of Education on January 9, 2026.

Merger Steering Committee

10–11 members: 2 secondary Perkins leads and 2 secondary administrators from each consortium, 1–2 postsecondary Perkins leads, and a local workforce representative.

Charge: Draft recommendations for aligning the CLNA, structuring the program advisory committee, and guiding committee actions through formal by-laws; manage feedback and finalize recommendations from consortium members.

Two work groups

Comprehensive Local Needs Assessment: review both current CLNAs, compare with best practices, and recommend how to align the 2026 CLNAs in anticipation of one combined assessment.

Program Advisory Committee Structure & Bylaws: review current informal protocols and other large consortia's bylaws to recommend policies and procedures for the new, combined consortium.

Process

How a consortium change officially happens

Minnesota State treats consortium changes as high-stakes decisions that require formal review at every step.

Step 1

Careful consideration

The impact on students, member institutions, and the consortium as a whole is weighed before any formal step is taken.

Step 2

Notice of Intent

Sent by the superintendent(s) or college president(s) to the State CTE Director, including a rationale for the change.

Step 3

Written acknowledgement & impact analysis

Every member superintendent and college president signs off, with an impact analysis covering finances, accountability (consortium performance indicators), availability of CTE programming, and consortium leadership/personnel/governance structure.

Ongoing

Joint planning

Dakota County and South Metro representatives, coordinators, district leaders, and CTE teachers continue drafting a shared governance structure for the future consortium.

Governance rules that shape the merger

  • Every consortium needs at least one eligible school district and one eligible college.
  • No school district or college may belong to more than one consortium — the core reason the merger is required.
  • Superintendents and the college president identify Perkins coordinators.
  • The combined consortium must submit a single annual funding application.
  • Spending authority rests with the defined leadership structure and cannot be delegated.

Minnesota's 23 Perkins consortia

The map of Minnesota Perkins consortia shows Dakota County and South Metro as neighboring, overlapping regions in the south metro — alongside Southwest Metro, Northeast Metro, Oak Land, Minneapolis, and St. Paul. Each of the state's 23 consortia pairs school districts with a Minnesota State college.

🕒

Where things stand

As of the FY26 Local Application, the merger is in active planning. ISD 917 is redesigning its CTE programs in anticipation of the change, and the consortium has committed FY26 funds to a full-time postsecondary Perkins Coordinator, a Postsecondary K–12 Director/Coordinator, and a part-time Secondary Perkins Coordinator — with a 1.0 FTE Secondary Coordinator under exploration for 2026–27 and beyond. DCTC and Inver Hills Community College are expected to fully merge as institutions by summer 2027.
